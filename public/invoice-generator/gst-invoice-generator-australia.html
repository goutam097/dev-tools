A tax invoice generally identifies the supplier and ABN, gives the issue date, describes each item and its quantity, and shows the price and GST treatment. For invoices at or above the applicable threshold, tax invoice requirements apply; buyer details can be required for higher-value invoices. The precise particulars and whether a document should be titled Tax Invoice depend on the circumstances.

For a simple standard-rated example, a GST-exclusive AUD 100 supply attracts AUD 10 GST and totals AUD 110. A GST-inclusive AUD 110 price contains AUD 10 GST. What to include on a Australia invoice

A tax invoice generally includes the supplier's identity and ABN, issue date, description and quantity, price, GST amount or a statement that the price includes GST, and total.

  • Supplier identity and ABN
  • Tax Invoice title for invoices of $82.50 or more
  • Issue date and description of each item
  • GST amount or clear GST-inclusive pricing
  • Total price and buyer details when required

Worked example

Worked example: three units at AUD 110 each with GST included total AUD 330. The embedded GST is AUD 30, leaving AUD 300 before tax. At an exclusive AUD 100 per unit, the same three units total AUD 330 after AUD 30 GST.

Common invoice mistakes to avoid

  • Adding GST again to a price that already includes it
  • Assuming every zero-GST item is treated the same way
  • Using an ABN number without checking its registration status
  • Forgetting buyer information needed for a higher-value tax invoice

No. Registration and supply classification matter; verify the treatment with current ATO guidance.
