A useful commercial invoice identifies the seller and customer, uses a unique invoice number and date, and itemizes the goods or services, quantity, price and taxes charged. Where registration details are required, show the GST/HST account number. Separately identifying applicable GST/HST and provincial tax can make the calculation easier for the customer to review.

What to include on a Canada invoice

Canadian GST/HST/PST treatment depends on the place of supply, province, and goods or services. A commercial invoice should clearly show supplier details, tax registration where applicable, and each tax charged.

  • Supplier and customer names and addresses
  • Invoice number and invoice date
  • Description, quantity and price of each supply
  • GST/HST registration number where required
  • Applicable GST/HST/PST rate and amount, itemized clearly

Worked example

Worked example: a CAD 200 taxable sale using the Ontario 13% preset shows CAD 26 tax and a CAD 226 total. The same CAD 200 sale using Alberta's 5% preset shows CAD 10 tax and a CAD 210 total. These examples assume the selected province and rate are correct for the supply.

Common invoice mistakes to avoid

  • Applying the seller's province instead of checking place-of-supply rules
  • Treating a province preset as a legal tax determination
  • Omitting a GST/HST account number when required
  • Forgetting separate provincial tax or special product rules
