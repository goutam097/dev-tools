United States Sales tax Invoice Generator

Create a US invoice with USD, a state and local sales-tax percentage field, and Letter paper as the default. The United States does not have a national VAT; state and local rules govern sales tax. A professional invoice usually shows seller and customer contact information, a unique invoice number, issue and due dates, a clear description of each item, quantities, unit prices and a total. If sales tax applies, identify the relevant jurisdiction and show the rate and tax amount clearly. Include payment terms and any purchase-order reference useful to the customer.

For illustration, a USD 100 taxable sale at a manually entered 8.25% rate adds USD 8.25 tax and totals USD 108.25. A different destination can have a different combined state, county, city or district rate. Taxability can also vary by product and customer exemption, so this arithmetic does not establish that the tax is due.

Check state and local rules for economic or physical nexus, sourcing, registration, filing and exemption documentation. Do not treat the optional EIN note as a substitute for a W-9 or official tax form. This generic invoice does not calculate jurisdiction-specific tax or provide tax advice; verify the applicable rate and invoice requirements with the relevant revenue authority.

What to include on a United States invoice

The United States has no national VAT. Sales tax rules vary by state and locality, and a seller may need to show local tax registration and tax separately.

  • Seller and customer contact details
  • Unique invoice number and invoice date
  • Clear description, quantity and price
  • State and local sales tax rate and amount when applicable
  • Payment terms and amount due

Worked example

Worked example: a USD 100 taxable service with an entered 8.25% rate has USD 8.25 tax and USD 108.25 due. If the transaction is not taxable, set the rate to zero only after confirming the applicable jurisdiction and exemption rules.

Common invoice mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming a single national sales-tax rate exists
  • Using a rate for the seller's address without checking sourcing rules
  • Charging tax without confirming registration and nexus
  • Treating the EIN note as a completed W-9

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculate US state tax automatically?

No. Enter the state or local rate yourself after checking the relevant jurisdiction.

Is there a national US VAT rate?

No. The United States does not have a national VAT; sales tax varies by jurisdiction.

Does it determine sales-tax nexus?

No. Nexus, sourcing, taxability and registration require a separate analysis.

What paper size is selected?

Letter is the default on the US page; A4 is also available.

Can I include an EIN or W-9 note?

Yes. An optional note field is available, but it does not replace a W-9.

Does the tax field verify the selected rate?

No. It only calculates the rate you enter and does not validate local rules.
