What to include on a European Union invoice

EU VAT invoice requirements depend on the member state and transaction. Cross-border B2B supplies may use reverse charge when the relevant conditions are met.

  • Supplier and customer names, addresses and VAT IDs
  • Sequential invoice number and invoice date
  • Supply date and description of goods or services
  • Taxable amount and applicable VAT rate
  • VAT amount or the applicable reverse-charge wording

Worked example

Worked example: at a 19% German standard-rate preset, a €100 net line has €19 VAT and totals €119. A separate 0% line remains €100 before any permitted adjustment. If a valid reverse-charge transaction applies, verify the prescribed wording and reporting treatment rather than relying solely on this preview.

Common invoice mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming one member state's standard rate applies throughout the EU
  • Using the reverse-charge option without confirming both parties and transaction qualify
  • Omitting a required supply date or VAT ID
  • Treating this generic page as a check of VAT ID validity

Frequently asked questions

Which countries have rate presets?

Germany, France, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands and Ireland are available as editable starting points.

Does the country picker select every applicable VAT rate?

No. It sets a standard-rate starting point; verify reduced and special rates for the actual supply.

Does reverse charge verify the transaction?

No. It adds explanatory wording only; eligibility and reporting must be checked separately.

Are VAT IDs validated online?

No. This offline page only performs a soft format check.

Can I enter tax-inclusive prices?

Yes. Switch the inclusive-pricing control on to extract tax from gross line prices.

Is this legal or tax advice?

No. Confirm requirements with the relevant member state's tax authority or an accountant.
